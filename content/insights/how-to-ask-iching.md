## The Short Answer

A good *I Ching* question does three things:

1. **It gives the situation, not just the question.** Two sentences of context — what is happening, where you are stuck — anchor the reading in your actual circumstances.
2. **It asks one thing.** Compound questions ("Should I take the job and move to Shanghai?") are really two questions and cannot be answered as one.
3. **It points at something you can do.** The classic form asks about your own next action, not about what other people will decide or what will happen to you.

That is the whole method. The rest of this guide shows what each move looks like in practice, with examples you can adapt.

## Move 1: Give the Situation, Not Just the Question

The same question means different things in different situations. "How can I improve communication with my partner?" is one question after a missed dinner; it is another question after ten years of avoidance. When you consult, include the specifics: what happened recently, what you have already tried, what you are afraid of.

You do not need to write an essay. On [GoEast's guided consultation](/consult/iching) the flow asks for exactly this — your topic, where you are stuck, and (optionally) what kind of insight you want — and assembles it into your question with you.

## Move 2: One Question at a Time

Split compound questions into their parts and ask them separately — ideally as separate consultations, because each deserves its own cast:

- ~~"Should I accept the offer and how do I tell my current boss?"~~ → Ask first: "What should I weigh in deciding whether to accept this offer?" Then, after you have decided: "How can I leave my current role with integrity?"
- ~~"Will the project succeed and should I change the team?"~~ → Ask first: "What is the strongest risk to this project right now?" Then act on what you learn.

## Move 3: Ask About Your Own Action

The *I Ching*'s tradition of advice — from the Zhouyi's judgments onward — is about timing and conduct: when to advance, when to wait, what to release. Questions about your own choices use that strength:

- ~~"Will my manager ever respect my work?"~~ → "What in my own conduct is undermining how my work is seen?"
- ~~"When will I be successful?"~~ → "What habit should I change first to build toward the work I want?"

## Eight Examples: Vague → Clearer → Why

These examples are original to this guide; adapt the wording to your situation rather than copying it.

**Work choices**

1. ~~"Should I quit my job?"~~ → **"I have been offered a role with more scope but less stability (context: two years in my current team, a new manager incoming). What should I weigh in deciding whether to stay or go?"**
   *Why:* it supplies the situation, asks one question, and keeps the decision — and the agency — with you.

2. ~~"Is this startup idea good?"~~ → **"I want to test my idea for a bilingual tutoring service within eight weeks while keeping my day job. What is the first assumption I should test, and what would count as evidence against it?"**
   *Why:* "good" is not actionable; a testable next step is. (See also [I Ching decision-making for entrepreneurs](/insights/iching-decision-making).)

**Interpersonal communication**

3. ~~"Why doesn't my mother listen to me?"~~ → **"Every call with my mother ends in the same argument about my moving abroad. What pattern am I repeating in these conversations, and what could I do differently in the next one?"**
   *Why:* it turns a complaint about someone else into a question about your own conduct — the part you control.

4. ~~"How do I fix my friendship with K?"~~ → **"My closest friend has gone quiet since I cancelled our trip. I miss the friendship but feel awkward reaching out first. What should I consider before I write to them?"**
   *Why:* context (the silence, the awkwardness) plus one concrete next action (the message).

**Creative habits**

5. ~~"Why can't I stick to my writing routine?"~~ → **"I start strong every Monday and abandon it by Thursday — three months running. What does my pattern of starting and stopping tell me about how I should structure the habit?"**
   *Why:* it names the observed pattern, which is exactly what a hexagram speaks to. (This is the situation behind our [complete worked example](/examples/iching-writing-habit).)

6. ~~"Will I ever finish my album?"~~ → **"I have nine of twelve songs demoed and keep polishing instead of recording the last three. What am I protecting myself from by not finishing?"**
   *Why:* "ever" invites prediction; the rewritten question asks about resistance you can examine.

**Personal growth**

7. ~~"Am I on the right path?"~~ → **"I left a stable career two years ago for independent work. Income is uneven but the work fits me. When I imagine returning to a salaried role, what am I actually afraid of?"**
   *Why:* "right path" outsources the judgment; the rewrite surfaces the real question underneath.

8. ~~"How do I become more disciplined?"~~ → **"I keep committing to morning practice and sleeping through it. Is the problem my evenings or my expectations of mornings — and what one change would test that?"**
   *Why:* it narrows a vague virtue ("discipline") to a testable hypothesis.

## Following Up on the Same Cast

A reading is the beginning of a conversation, not the end. On GoEast, after you receive a reading you can [ask follow-up questions about the same cast](/consult/iching) — the same hexagram, the same changing lines, the same original reading — up to twenty follow-ups per reading. Follow-ups are good for:

- "What does the changing line mean for my situation specifically?"
- "You described waiting as favorable here — what does waiting look like concretely for me?"
- "Does this reading change how I should frame the conversation with my manager?"

**When to cast anew instead:** start a *new* consultation when the situation itself has changed — the decision is made, the conversation happened, a genuinely new question has emerged — or when follow-ups on the old cast keep circling without adding anything. A new question deserves a new cast; a deeper question about the same situation belongs on the same cast.

(For what changing lines are and how they transform a hexagram, see [I Ching changing lines, explained](/insights/iching-changing-lines).)

## What the I Ching Will Not Do

The *I Ching* — and any honest reader of it — does not predict specific outcomes, and it is not a substitute for professional help:

- It does not tell you whether an event will happen.
- It does not replace medical, legal, or financial advice from qualified professionals.
- On GoEast, readings are framed as material for reflection; the AI interpretation is labeled as AI-generated.
