# Top 10 Apps You Need Before Visiting China in 2026

China runs on mobile apps. Cash is nearly extinct, paper maps are relics, and even street vendors expect digital payment. If you arrive without the right apps installed and configured, your first 48 hours will be frustrating.

This guide covers the 10 essential apps every foreign traveler needs, in priority order. Set up the first four **before you leave home**.

## 1. Alipay (支付宝) — Your Primary Wallet

Alipay is the most foreigner-friendly payment app in China. In 2026, it supports passport verification and international card linking without a Chinese bank account.

**Why it's #1:** Broader acceptance for city services, utility payments, and transport. The built-in mini-programs cover everything from metro tickets to hospital registrations.

**Setup tips:**
- Download the international version (blue icon, "Alipay")
- Complete identity verification with your passport — do this 2-3 days before travel
- Link your international credit/debit card
- Make a test transaction before arriving

See our detailed guide: [Alipay vs WeChat Pay comparison](/insights/alipay-vs-wechat-pay-foreigner).

## 2. WeChat (微信) — Communication + Backup Payment

WeChat is how China communicates. You'll need it to contact hotel staff, restaurant reservations, tour guides, and virtually anyone you meet.

**Why essential:** WeChat Pay is the backup when Alipay has issues. Many small vendors prefer WeChat Pay. Hotels and restaurants will add you to group chats for coordination.

**Setup tips:**
- Register with your international phone number before leaving
- Enable WeChat Pay and link a card
- Add a few contacts or join expat groups

See our guide: [WeChat Pay for foreigners](/insights/wechat-pay-foreigner).

## 3. Amap / Gaode Maps (高德地图) — Navigation

Google Maps does not work reliably in China. Amap (Gaode) is the gold standard for navigation, covering driving, walking, public transit, and real-time traffic.

**Why essential:** Accurate POI data, real-time bus and subway schedules, and walking navigation that actually works indoors (malls, train stations).

**Language barrier:** The app is in Chinese. Take screenshots of key addresses in Chinese characters before you go, then paste them into the search bar.

**Alternative:** Apple Maps works in China and shows English labels in major cities. For Android users, Amap is your best option.

## 4. eSIM or VPN App — Stay Connected

You need reliable internet from the moment you land. Two approaches:

**eSIM (recommended):** Services like Airalo, Holafly, or China Unicom tourist eSIMs give you data immediately without swapping physical SIMs. See our [eSIM guide for foreigners](/insights/china-esim-foreigner-guide).

**VPN:** China's internet is filtered. If you need Gmail, Google, WhatsApp, or Western social media, set up a VPN before arrival. See our [VPN travel guide](/insights/china-vpn-travel-guide).

**Key rule:** Download and test your VPN **before** entering China. Installing one from within China is unreliable.

## 5. DiDi (滴滴出行) — Ride-Hailing

DiDi is China's Uber. It's dramatically cheaper than taxis, eliminates language barriers (destination is entered digitally), and provides trip records.

**Why essential:** Taxis can be hard to hail, and explaining destinations in Chinese is difficult. DiDi handles everything in the app, including estimated fares and driver communication.

**English support:** DiDi has an English interface within the Alipay mini-program. See our [DiDi English guide](/insights/didi-english-guide-china).

## 6. Translation App — Break the Language Barrier

You'll encounter menus, signs, and conversations in Chinese constantly. A good translation app is your lifeline.

**Top picks:**
- **Pleco** — The best Chinese dictionary app, with camera OCR for reading signs and menus
- **Google Translate** (download offline Chinese pack before arrival) — Conversation mode works well
- **DeepL** — Excellent for written text translation
- **Baidu Translate** — Works within China without VPN

See our full comparison: [Best translation apps for China travel](/insights/best-translation-apps-china-travel).

## 7. Meituan (美团) — Food Delivery + Local Services

Meituan is the everything-app for food delivery, restaurant reviews, movie tickets, hotel bookings, and local services.

**Why useful:** Even if you don't speak Chinese, the photo menus and review ratings help you choose restaurants. Food delivery works with Alipay/WeChat Pay.

**Tip:** Your hotel concierge can help set up a delivery address. See our [food delivery guide](/insights/china-food-delivery-foreigner-guide).

## 8. Trip.com (携程) — Train Tickets + Hotels

Trip.com is the English-friendly version of Ctrip. Use it for high-speed rail tickets, domestic flights, and hotel bookings.

**Why essential:** China's 12306 train booking system is in Chinese and requires verification. Trip.com handles this for a small fee and provides English support.

See our [12306 high-speed rail guide](/insights/china-high-speed-rail-12306-guide) for when to book directly.

## 9. Xiaohongshu (小红书) — Local Discovery

Xiaohongshu (Little Red Book) is China's Instagram-me-review-platform. It's invaluable for discovering restaurants, attractions, and hidden gems that don't appear on Western travel sites.

**How to use it:** Search for your destination in Chinese (or use the camera translation feature) and browse photo reviews. Many posts include practical tips that guidebooks miss.

## 10. Hospital/Medical Apps — Emergency Preparedness

If you need medical care, having the right app ready saves critical time.

**Options:**
- **Ping An Good Doctor (平安好医生)** — Online consultation in English
- **WeChat mini-programs** — Many hospitals have appointment booking mini-programs
- **International SOS** — If your insurance includes it

See our [hospital guide for foreigners](/insights/china-hospital-foreigner-guide).

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Waiting until arrival to set up payment apps.** Verification takes 24-72 hours. Do it at home.
2. **Relying solely on Google Maps.** Download Amap or ensure Apple Maps works for your destinations.
3. **Skipping VPN setup before departure.** Installing VPNs from within China is unreliable.
4. **Only setting up one payment app.** Merchants sometimes only accept one or the other. Have both.
5. **Not testing apps with a real transaction.** Make a small test payment before you travel.

## Final Thoughts

China's digital ecosystem is self-contained and incredibly efficient once you're set up. The upfront effort of installing and configuring these 10 apps will transform your travel experience from frustrating to seamless.

Start with Alipay and WeChat — they're the foundation everything else builds on. Once those work, the rest falls into place quickly.
